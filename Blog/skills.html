Drum

Paul started playing the drums at a very young age. He enjoyed watching the drummer at his church play, and he decided he would try to play like him, too. However, he didn't take drumming as a serious passion until late middle school, where he started to dedicate time to improving his skills, as well as playing in the school's band ensemble. Additionally, he played at church, furthering his skills the more.

Basketball

This is one of Paul's stranger hobbies to explain. Basketball was not his first sport to love--it was actually hockey. In fact, it was quite literally until high school that he started to actually play basketball. When he started, he found a lot of joy in shooting the ball and running around. He discovered that he had an exceptionally high vertical jump, and it made him an excellently skilled basketball player. However, basketball and sports was not his biggest passion, so he never pursued it, despite several encouragements from peers.

Math

Since birth, Paul loved to do math. This was something he had confidence in, since the moment he could count. He learned math so early, that he was able to skip an entire grade. He loves doing equations in his head, he loves working with numbers and making them do things. It can be assumed that his love for math is connected to his love for coding.

Minecraft

While a video game may seem out of place in a list of useful, profitable skills, Minecraft had a major impact on Paul and his journey.


The sandbox game comes in different versions and is different from any other game because of the possibilities it holds. Some choose to play the game as a game to survive, some to build masterpieces, and others to create machines and code things into the game. Paul was part of all of these categories, but mostly the latter. He started playing minecraft when the education edition was made available on the school devices. The education edition featured a built-in interface where students like him could move blocks of code to create sequences that would perform actions in the game. It was the perfect way for him to experience the world of code and learn how to solve problems.



Paul repeatedly acknowledges that Minecraft was the biggest reason he got into coding. As he grew up, he kept coming back to Minecraft and made more masterpieces. Everytime he came back, he would create something better than the last. It almost seemed like there were more options being added every time, but in reality, Paul was just getting better and better.
